\noindent\textbf{\large Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{section}{Abstract}
\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Accurate spatio-temporal forecasting of ride-hailing demand is a cornerstone of modern Intelligent Transportation Systems. In this project we reimplement, validate, and optimize the \textbf{Local Perception-Enhanced Spatial-Temporal Evolving Graph Transformer Network} (LPE-STGTN), a state-of-the-art architecture for citywide demand prediction published in IEEE TITS 2024. We apply the model to the NYC Yellow Taxi 2018 dataset (67 Manhattan zones, 15-minute resolution, 86 million trips) and progress through three experimental phases: paper-aligned reproduction (MAPE 35.66\%), topological corrections (Zone 104 removal, $\epsilon$-sparsification), and exhaustive ablation studies. Our final configuration achieves a test MAPE of \textbf{32.41\%}, MAE of \textbf{7.12}, and RMSE of \textbf{12.09}, closing the gap to the paper target (31.82\%) to just \textbf{0.59 percentage points}.

\vspace{0.3em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} spatio-temporal forecasting, graph neural networks, ride-hailing demand, attention-free transformer, graph sparsification, ablation study

%============================================================
\section{Introduction and Paper Summary}
%============================================================

\subsection{Motivation}
As ride-hailing platforms become embedded in modern urban mobility, the ability to accurately anticipate passenger demand is central to optimizing fleet dispatching, reducing empty cruising, and minimizing congestion. Classical time-series models treat each zone independently, losing the spatial structure that defines a city. Graph-based deep learning offers a principled way to encode these geographic relationships directly into the forecasting model.

\subsection{Paper Summary}
The paper by Zhang et al. (IEEE TITS, 2024) proposes the LPE-STGTN, a Transformer-based architecture designed specifically for citywide taxi and ride-hailing demand prediction. The key innovation is a dual-pathway design:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Local Perception Path:} An Attention-Free Transformer (AFT) captures fine-grained temporal patterns within a sliding window of size $W{=}4$, avoiding the quadratic cost of standard self-attention. Its output feeds into a Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network (GCRN) that propagates spatial information along graph edges at each recurrent step.
    \item \textbf{Global Semantic Fusion Path:} Two separate graph convolutions are applied over a distance-based adjacency graph and an Origin-Destination (OD) flow graph. The resulting feature sets are merged via multi-head attention (4 heads) to capture city-wide semantic relationships.
\end{itemize}

The authors evaluate on NYC Taxi and Bike-sharing datasets. For the NYC experiment, they report 68 Manhattan TLC zones, 15-minute aggregation, 12-step input/output horizons, and achieve MAE 5.66, RMSE 10.40, and MAPE 31.82\%. Training uses L1 loss, Adam optimizer (lr $10^{-3}$), batch size 64, hidden dimension 64, and 1 GCRN layer.

\subsection{Problem Definition}
Given an urban graph $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$ where $\mathcal{V}$ represents $N$ geographic zones and a history of demand observations over the past $T$ time steps, the task is to predict demand for the next $P{=}12$ steps (3 hours ahead).

\subsection{Our Contributions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item A faithful end-to-end reimplementation of the LPE-STGTN architecture in PyTorch, from raw data ingestion through graph construction to full model training on a GPU cluster.
    \item Identification and resolution of a critical geographic anomaly (TLC Zone 104) that injects ${\sim}3{,}860$ feet of phantom distance into the spatial adjacency matrix.
    \item An extensive ablation study covering network capacity, dropout regularization, $\epsilon$-threshold sparsification, weather feature integration, and chronological split sensitivity.
    \item A systematic hyperparameter sweep yielding a final test MAPE of 32.41\%, reducing the gap to the paper target from 3.84 pp to 0.59 pp.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Model Architecture Description}
%============================================================

The LPE-STGTN processes historical demand through two parallel pathways - a \emph{local} perception path and a \emph{global} semantic path - before fusing their outputs to produce multi-step predictions.

\subsection{Overall Pipeline}
The input is a tensor of shape $(B, T, N)$ representing $B$ samples, each containing $T{=}12$ historical time steps across $N$ zones. The pipeline proceeds as follows:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Embedding:} Raw demand, Time-of-Day, and Day-of-Week features are concatenated and linearly projected to dimension $d$.
    \item \textbf{Local Path:} AFT-Local extracts temporal features within a sliding window, followed by GCRN for spatial propagation.
    \item \textbf{Global Path:} Dual graph convolutions over distance and OD graphs, fused via multi-head attention.
    \item \textbf{Fusion:} Local and global representations are combined and decoded into the 12-step forecast.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Spatio-Temporal Embedding}
Raw demand values are concatenated with Time-of-Day and Day-of-Week features, then projected into a continuous latent space of dimension $d$ via a linear layer. This ensures the model incorporates cyclical temporal priors before any convolutional extraction.

\subsection{Local Perception Path}
\textbf{Attention-Free Transformer (AFT):} The AFT replaces standard dot-product attention with element-wise position biases within a local sliding window $W{=}4$:
\begin{equation}
Y_{\text{AFT}} = \sigma(Q) \odot \frac{\sum_{t'=1}^{W} \exp(K_{t'} + w_{t'}) \odot V_{t'}}{\sum_{t'=1}^{W} \exp(K_{t'} + w_{t'})}
\end{equation}

This design is critical: it provides $O(T \cdot W)$ complexity instead of the $O(T^2)$ cost of standard self-attention, making it efficient for high-frequency (15-min) urban data.

\textbf{GCRN:} The AFT output feeds into a single-layer Graph Convolutional Recurrent Network that performs graph convolution at each recurrent step, allowing spatial information to flow along the adjacency graph while maintaining temporal state through GRU gates.

\subsection{Global Semantic Fusion Path}
Graph convolution is applied separately over $\mathcal{G}_{\text{dist}}$ (distance graph) and $\mathcal{G}_{\text{OD}}$ (Origin-Destination flow graph). The two feature sets are fused via token-wise multi-head attention (4 heads), allowing the model to learn which spatial relationships are most informative at each time step.

\subsection{Output and Loss Function}
The fused representation is decoded through a linear output layer to produce the 12-step forecast. Training minimizes the mean L1 (absolute error) loss between predicted and actual demand values across all zones and time steps, optimized with Adam (initial learning rate $10^{-3}$). A \texttt{ReduceLROnPlateau} scheduler halves the learning rate when validation loss stagnates.

\subsection{Framework and Environment}
The entire codebase is implemented in \textbf{Python 3.9} using \textbf{PyTorch 2.x} as the deep learning framework. Graph construction relies on NetworkX and OSMnx for road-network shortest-path computation. All training runs were executed on the university GPU cluster equipped with an \textbf{NVIDIA L40S} (46 GB VRAM).

\subsection{Key Implementation Decisions}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Graph snapping:} Zone centroids are snapped to the nearest driveable OSM node, not to arbitrary coordinates. This ensures that pairwise shortest-path distances reflect actual road-network distances.
    \item \textbf{Symmetric normalization:} Both adjacency matrices use $\tilde{A} = I + D^{-1/2}AD^{-1/2}$ to ensure stable spectral properties during graph convolution.
    \item \textbf{MAPE zero-masking:} Zones with ground-truth demand $< 1$ trip are excluded from MAPE computation to prevent explosive relative errors during low-demand periods (late night).
    \item \textbf{Gradient clipping:} Initially applied (max norm 5.0) following common practice, but later removed after ablation showed it marginally degraded MAPE.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experimental Setup}
%============================================================

\subsection{Dataset}
We use the \textbf{NYC Yellow Taxi 2018} trip-record dataset published by the NYC TLC. The raw data comprises 12 monthly Apache Parquet files. After filtering to trips whose pickup \emph{and} dropoff both fall within the Manhattan study area (``paper-strict'' definition), the processed dataset retains \textbf{86{,}442{,}571} trips - only 38 fewer than the paper's reported 86{,}442{,}609.

\subsection{Preprocessing and Graph Construction}
All pickups are binned into \textbf{15-minute} intervals, yielding 96 time steps per day and 35{,}040 steps over the full year. A Z-score normalization is applied using training-set statistics only ($\hat{x} = (x - \mu_{\text{train}}) / \sigma_{\text{train}}$) to prevent temporal leakage.

Two spatial graphs are constructed: a \textbf{Distance Graph} using Gaussian-kernel-transformed shortest-path road distances (Eq.~\ref{eq:dist_kernel}), and an \textbf{OD Flow Graph} using squared z-scored trip counts between zone pairs.

\begin{equation}
A_{ij}^{\text{dist}} = \exp\!\left(-\frac{d_{ij}^{2}}{\sigma^{2}}\right)
\label{eq:dist_kernel}
\end{equation}

\section{Analysis and Discussion}
%============================================================

\subsection{Key Findings}

\textbf{Data quality dominates architecture.} The single largest improvement in test MAPE did not come from increasing hidden dimensions or adding attention heads, but from removing a single geographically disconnected zone (Zone 104) and applying $\epsilon$-threshold sparsification. In graph neural networks, a single anomalous node can propagate gradient noise through the entire message-passing pipeline.

\textbf{Sparsification exhibits a clear optimum.} The $\epsilon$-sweep (Fig.~\ref{fig:pareto}) reveals a U-shaped error curve. At $\epsilon{=}0.00$ (fully dense), the attention module wastes capacity modeling near-zero edges. At $\epsilon{=}0.10$ (aggressively sparse), the model loses access to mid-range neighbors with genuine predictive signal. The optimum at $\epsilon{=}0.05$ prunes approximately 75\% of edges while preserving meaningful connections.

\textbf{Weather features degrade generalization.} Injecting Open-Meteo features consistently worsened all metrics by 0.9-2.6 percentage points. Time-of-Day and Day-of-Week cyclic embeddings already implicitly encode seasonal patterns; adding weather vectors introduces noise without proportional information gain.

\textbf{Training data volume is critical.} Reducing the training set from 60\% to 20\% degrades MAPE by over 1.7 pp, confirming that spatio-temporal graph models require substantial historical coverage to learn robust temporal patterns.

\subsection{Remaining Gap Analysis}

Our best configuration leaves a residual gap of 0.59 pp to the paper's 31.82\%. We identify three plausible sources: (1) undisclosed preprocessing steps in the original paper (our 86{,}442{,}571 trips vs their 86{,}442{,}609 suggest minor filtering differences), (2) unspecified graph construction thresholds (our $\epsilon$-sweep shows 0.5-1.0 pp sensitivity), and (3) single-seed vs multi-seed averaging (seed-to-seed variance can account for 0.3-0.5 pp).

\subsection{Hyperparameter Sensitivity}
The ablation studies reveal that the model is most sensitive to graph topology ($\epsilon$-threshold) and dropout rate, and least sensitive to hidden dimension changes above 64. This hierarchy of sensitivity - graph structure $>$ regularization $>$ capacity - is an important practical insight for deploying similar architectures.

\subsection{Limitations}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Exogenous event blindness.} Holidays, severe weather, and public events violate cyclical assumptions. A dedicated event embedding could address this.
    \item \textbf{Scalability constraints.} Extending from Manhattan ($N{=}67$) to all NYC boroughs ($N{\approx}265$) would increase attention cost by ${\sim}15{\times}$.
    \item \textbf{MAPE instability.} The zero-masking threshold ($y < 1$) directly affects reported MAPE values.
    \item \textbf{Static graph assumption.} Both graphs are computed from full-year aggregates and remain fixed during training, missing weekday/weekend OD shifts.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion and Lessons Learned}
%============================================================

\subsection{Summary}
This report presents a complete reimplementation and optimization of the LPE-STGTN architecture for citywide ride-hailing demand forecasting. Starting from over 86 million raw NYC taxi trips, we built a config-driven preprocessing pipeline, constructed dual spatial graphs from real road-network data, and deployed the full model on a GPU cluster. Through three systematic phases - paper-aligned reproduction, topological correction, and exhaustive ablation - we improved test MAPE from 35.66\% to \textbf{32.41\%}, closing the gap to the published benchmark (31.82\%) to just 0.59 percentage points.

\subsection{Lessons Learned}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Reproducibility is harder than expected.} The paper omits several critical details (zone count, graph thresholds, MAPE handling, gradient clipping). Each ambiguity required careful experimentation to resolve, consuming significant engineering time.
    \item \textbf{Data quality matters more than model complexity.} The single biggest performance gain came from removing one anomalous zone, not from doubling the hidden dimension.
    \item \textbf{Graph topology is a first-class hyperparameter.} The $\epsilon$-sparsification sweep showed that graph structure is as important to tune as learning rate or dropout.
    \item \textbf{Config-driven pipelines are essential.} Running 15+ experiments across different data configurations, hyperparameters, and graph variants would have been unmanageable without YAML-based experiment tracking.
    \item \textbf{GPU debugging is a real skill.} Zombie GPU processes, CUDA memory leaks, and deadlocks from stale file locks required systematic diagnosis during long training runs.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Future Work}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
    \item \textbf{Multi-city transfer learning:} Evaluating whether a model pre-trained on NYC transfers to other cities with minimal fine-tuning.
    \item \textbf{Dynamic temporal graphs:} Replacing static annual OD aggregates with time-varying graphs that adapt to weekday/weekend patterns.
    \item \textbf{Event-aware embeddings:} Incorporating holiday calendars and real-time weather alerts as structured inputs.
    \item \textbf{Scalable attention:} Implementing hierarchical attention to enable deployment across all NYC boroughs.
\end{enumerate}
